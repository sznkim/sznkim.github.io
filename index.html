About

I am an MPH candidate at the SNU Health Economics I Lab. My research examines how older adults and their families adjust medical care, insurance, and economic resources after a health shock. I use longitudinal surveys and large-scale administrative data with applied microeconometric and machine-learning methods.

Previously, I worked in the World Bank Office of the Chief Economist for Infrastructure, supporting cross-country research and reproducible data workflows across more than 150 countries.

  • How does one spouse’s health shock change the other spouse’s healthcare use, insurance decisions, and allocation of household resources?
  • Why do some households recover from medical financial burden while others experience persistent or recurring hardship?
  • When do health insurance and cost-sharing policies help families maintain necessary care after a health shock?

Future research directions

  • Household responses to acute health shocks: Compare healthcare use before and after less predictable hospitalizations to examine how care and resources are reallocated within older households.
  • Differences and dynamics: Identify how responses vary with household resources and insurance coverage, and whether early changes in care are temporary or persist in later health and medical spending.
  • Insurance and cost sharing: Use policy-induced variation in out-of-pocket costs to distinguish financial constraints from responses to newly salient health risks and to measure policy effects beyond the directly treated patient.
